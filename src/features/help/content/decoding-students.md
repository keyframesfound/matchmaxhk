# Decoding Students: Motivation, Interventions, and SEN

Tutors do not diagnose ADHD, dyslexia, anxiety, burnout, or any other condition. Profiles below describe observed learning patterns and practical tutoring responses based on established educational psychology frameworks.

## Self-Determination Theory (SDT) & Student Motivation

The most pervasive challenge encountered is not a deficit in student intellect, but a deficit in student motivation. Motivation is dictated by the satisfaction or frustration of three universal, innate psychological needs:

- **Autonomy:** Provide meaningful rationales for assignments, avoid coercive language, and offer bounded choices (e.g., allowing the student to select the sequence of topics to review during a session) to foster a sense of self-endorsement.
- **Competence:** Maintain tasks within the optimal challenge zone (the Goldilocks Zone). If a task is too simplistic, the student experiences boredom; if overwhelmingly complex, they experience anxiety and task avoidance. Ensure the student consistently experiences the friction of challenge followed by the reward of mastery.
- **Relatedness:** Validate difficulties unconditionally. Track the student's extracurricular activities and integrate them into casual conversation. When a student feels viewed holistically, they are significantly more likely to take cognitive risks and admit when they do not understand.

## Student Profiles and Interventions

| Student profile | Likely root cause | MatchMax intervention strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Achiever aiming for 5\*\* / IB 7** | Needs advanced synthesis and may be bored by standard drill. The *Expertise Reversal Effect* demonstrates that intensive scaffolding actually inhibits learning for advanced students. | **Inverted GRR Model:** Skip basic explanations. Present a complex, un-scaffolded problem to activate prior knowledge, probe edge cases, and demand speed efficiency. |
| **Task Avoidant / High Anxiety** | Depleted self-efficacy and fear of failure disguised as "laziness" or "I can't do this". | **Micro-Scaffolding:** Break the problem into basic, infinitesimally small components. Secure three consecutive micro-successes to rehabilitate competence beliefs before increasing difficulty. |
| **ADHD/ADD** | Working memory fatigue or sensory overload. | **Dopamine Structuring:** Use Pomodoro-style timing, such as 25 minutes focus and 5 minutes kinetic break. Minimize visual clutter by covering upcoming questions. |
| **Dyslexia** | Phonological processing difficulties. | **Visual Restructuring:** Avoid text-heavy slides, use sans-serif fonts, allow verbal answers for concept checks, and use color overlays for reading comprehension. |
| **Burnout Meltdown** | Severe clinical exhaustion before DSE/IB mocks, including crying or panic attacks. | **Pivot Protocol:** Stop academic instruction, prioritize psychological safety, and gently loop parents in regarding observed exhaustion without breaching private trust. |

## Do/Avoid Checklist

**Do:** Offer structured choices, keep tasks inside the challenge zone, validate difficulty, break hard tasks into smaller actions, secure small wins, watch for fatigue/shutdown, use observed behavior rather than diagnostic labels.

**Avoid:** Calling students lazy, diagnosing conditions, overloading worksheets, continuing academic pressure during a meltdown, sharing private student comments carelessly, turning parent updates into criticism.

## Micro-Scaffolding Demonstration

Micro-scaffolding reduces avoidance by turning a large task into visible, achievable progress.

*Example Task: Write a 1000-word IB essay*

| Micro-step | Student action | Tutor support |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Choose the exact question | Offer two suitable options if stuck |
| 2 | Write 1 thesis sentence | Provide sentence stem if needed |
| 3 | List 3 evidence bullet points | Accept rough phrasing first |
| 4 | Match each evidence point to one explanation | Use arrows or color coding |
| 5 | Write one PEEL+ paragraph | Model the first sentence only if needed |
| 6 | Add linking sentence | Prompt connection to thesis |
| 7 | Repeat paragraph structure | Fade support |
| 8 | Review against marking objective | Check with criteria language |
