A clear request is the fastest route to a good match. Coordinators can only match against what you tell them, so this page covers what to include, what happens after you post, and what to have ready when introductions start.

## What a strong request contains

Write your request as if you were briefing a friend who knows tutors. The details that matter most:

- **Subjects and level.** Name the subjects and your child's year or form, for example "Secondary 3 maths" or "Year 10 physics".
- **Curriculum.** Say which curriculum your child follows: DSE, IB, IGCSE, or A-Level. Tutors specialise, and a DSE specialist is not automatically the right fit for IB. If you are weighing options, see [how the curricula compare](/help/parent-support/compare-ib-dse-igcse-and-a-level-curricula).
- **Lesson frequency.** How many lessons per week you want, and how long each should be.
- **Preferred times.** Weekday evenings, weekend mornings, or whatever suits your family's routine.
- **Format.** Face-to-face / in-home lessons, Zoom, or either.
- **Budget range.** A realistic range rather than a single number gives the coordinator room to find a better fit. Our [pricing page](/pricing) can help you calibrate.

One or two lines about your child's goals also help: catching up, building confidence, or pushing for top grades.

## What happens after you post

1. **Coordinator review.** A human coordinator reads your request, checks it is complete, and follows up promptly if anything is unclear. There is no fixed response clock, so replies come as soon as the coordinator is available.
2. **Matched introductions.** The coordinator introduces you to self-employed tutors whose specialism, curriculum experience, and availability fit your request.
3. **Your choice.** You compare the introduced tutors and pick the one you want. If the first introductions are not right, say so and the coordinator keeps looking.

The first two lessons are the supported concierge window. If the fit is not right in that period, tell the coordinator and other tutors can be introduced. To understand how this works overall, read [how MatchMax matching works](/help/parent-getting-started/how-matchmax-matching-works).

## What to prepare before introductions

A little preparation makes the first conversations easier:

- Your child's recent schoolwork or a topic list, so you can show the tutor where they are.
- A short list of times that genuinely work in a typical week.
- Questions you want to ask the tutor. [Choosing the right tutor for your child](/help/parent-getting-started/choose-the-right-tutor-for-your-child) includes a list you can borrow from.
- A payment method you are comfortable with, since you pay your tutor directly for tuition by FPS, bank transfer, or cash. MatchMax never holds tuition payments, and parents never pay MatchMax a matching fee or commission. See [pay your tutor directly and billing cycles](/help/parent-payments/pay-your-tutor-directly-and-billing-cycles) for how this works.
