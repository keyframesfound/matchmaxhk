Parents who can see progress stay for the long term. After every lesson, send the parent a short update: three or four lines is enough. The Rule of 3 keeps it consistent, and the template below means it takes two minutes to write.

## The Rule of 3

Every update covers the same three things:

1. **What the task was, in plain English.** Translate jargon for parents. SBA, TOK, Paper 1, or internal assessment terms mean little to most families; say what the student actually did, such as "practised structuring an essay argument" rather than "worked on Paper 1 response criteria".
2. **Why it matters.** Connect the task to the final grade or the school goal. One sentence is enough: this skill is worth marks in the exam, or it unblocks the next topic.
3. **The exact measurable next step.** Name the homework, the deadline, and what next lesson will focus on. "Practise more" is not a next step; "complete two past-paper questions on probability by Friday" is.

A personalised written summary addressed to both parent and student works best. Include specific, measurable success criteria and clear homework, so everyone knows what progress looks like before the next session.

## Copy-paste post-lesson template

```script
# Post-lesson update (Rule of 3)
--en
Hi [Parent Name] and [Student Name]! Quick recap of today's lesson. Covered: [Topic]. Progress: [Student Name] could [Specific Task] independently; [Skill] still needs support, so we will keep practising. Next step: complete [Homework] by [Date]; next lesson we focus on [Priority]. If anything new comes up at school, share it here beforehand so I can prepare!
--zh
[家長稱謂]、[同學名] 你哋好！今日課堂小結：學習內容係 [課題]；[同學名] 今日已經可以獨立完成 [具體任務]，做得好！[技巧] 暫時仲需要少少提示，我哋會繼續練習。下一步：請喺 [日期] 前完成 [功課]，下堂集中 [重點]。如果學校有新內容，歡迎上堂前喺度話我知！
```

## The 15-minute study-plan chat after Lesson 1

After the first lesson, offer a short chat of about 15 minutes with the parent (and the student where possible) to agree goals. Use it to:

- Agree two or three concrete goals for the term, with measurable markers of success.
- Share the written study plan with the parent so expectations are on the record.
- Propose a regular weekly slot without pressure. Suggest a time that works for you and let them confirm or counter; do not treat the suggestion itself as a booking. Confirmation works best through the flow in [Confirm lesson dates, reschedules, and tutor sick days](/help/scheduling-logistics/coordinate-lesson-dates).

## Keeping the feedback loop alive

Invite the parent to share school updates, test dates, and new topics before lessons so you can prepare. This turns your update into a two-way channel and makes the tutoring feel like a partnership. When parents see you acting on their input, renewals take care of themselves.
