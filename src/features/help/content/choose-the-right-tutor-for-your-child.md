Once matched introductions arrive, the choice is yours. This guide explains how to read a tutor card, what the verification tiers mean, and the questions worth asking before Lesson 1.

## Reading a tutor card

Each tutor card shows who the tutor is, what they teach, and how their credentials have been checked. Three things on the card tell you the most:

### Verification tiers

- **Blue Tick.** The tutor's identity and core documents have passed MatchMax verification. This is the baseline trust marker on the platform.
- **Verified Professionals tier.** Tutors whose professional credentials have been verified to a higher standard, alongside the Blue Tick checks.
- **Stated Qualifications tier.** Tutors who list qualifications that are shown as stated on their profile. The claims are visible but sit below the verified tiers, so it is fair to ask for proof before Lesson 1.

For how document checks work behind these tiers, read [how MatchMax verifies tutors](/help/parent-verification/how-matchmax-verifies-tutors).

### Curriculum specialism

Match the tutor's specialism to your child's curriculum before anything else. A DSE specialist knows the marking scheme and past-paper rhythm that HKDSE students need. IB tutors work with internal assessments and inquiry-based marking. IGCSE and A-Level tutors know the exam boards and their differences. A tutor who lists your child's curriculum as a specialism is usually a better fit than a stronger generalist in a different system. If you are still deciding between systems, see [how the curricula compare](/help/parent-support/compare-ib-dse-igcse-and-a-level-curricula).

### Experience signals

Look for concrete signals rather than years alone: the levels and forms taught, exam-board familiarity, and whether the tutor describes how they adapt lessons. MatchMax verifies tutor documents, but it does not guarantee grades, school offers, or results, so treat experience signals as questions to explore in conversation, not promises.

## Rapport with your child's age

A brilliant A-Level tutor can be a poor match for a Primary 4 child. When comparing tutors, check who they normally teach. Younger children usually need a tutor who is patient, warm, and comfortable keeping attention with short, varied activities. Teenagers preparing for public exams usually need structure and direct feedback. Neither style is better; the fit is with your child's age and temperament.

## The trial period is for testing fit

The first two lessons exist so you can see the fit before committing to a weekly schedule. Use them deliberately: watch how the tutor explains a hard concept, whether your child engages, and whether the tutor adapts when something does not land. If it is not working, tell the coordinator during this window and other tutors can be introduced. After the window, you agree a regular weekly schedule directly with your tutor. For the practical setup, see [prepare for your first lesson](/help/parent-scheduling/prepare-for-your-first-lesson).

## Questions to ask a tutor before Lesson 1

A short conversation before the first lesson settles most doubts. Worth asking:

- Can you share proof of the qualifications listed on your profile? This is a reasonable ask, especially for tutors in the Stated Qualifications tier.
- How do you approach the first lesson with a new student?
- What materials, such as past papers or your child's schoolwork, should we have ready?
- How do you report progress to parents between lessons?
- What is your rescheduling practice if a lesson has to move?

Keep [lesson safety and supervision guidelines](/help/parent-safety/lesson-safety-and-supervision-guidelines) in mind for in-home lessons, and remember you pay your tutor directly by FPS, bank transfer, or cash, with no matching fee or commission to MatchMax from parents.
