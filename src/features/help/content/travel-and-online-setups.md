How a lesson is delivered is part of the deal you make with each family. MatchMax supports Face-to-face / in-home tutoring, lessons at a tuition centre, and online lessons over Zoom, and it is up to you and the parent to agree which mode fits the student before the first session. This guide covers how to run each mode well, from travel planning to a Zoom setup that never wastes Lesson 1 on a microphone problem.

## Agree the delivery mode up front

Confirm with the parent, before you accept the case, whether lessons will be at your home, at the student's home, at a centre, or on Zoom. Put the decision in writing in your first WhatsApp message so there is no ambiguity later. If you are unsure how to open that conversation, see [Introduce yourself on WhatsApp](/help/parent-communication/introduce-yourself-whatsapp).

## In-home and Face-to-face lessons

Travelling to a student's home is the most common arrangement in Hong Kong, and it lives or dies on logistics:

- **Arrive 5 to 7 minutes early.** Early enough to set up, not so early that you are standing in the family's living room while they finish dinner.
- **Confirm the address and building access in advance.** Ask for the estate name, block, floor, and whether the guard needs a name at the desk or a lift intercom call. A wrong flat number costs you 10 minutes of goodwill.
- **Keep travel time realistic when accepting cases.** A 45-minute lesson across two MTR transfers is a 90-minute commitment. Factor travel into your schedule before you say yes, and if the timing stops working, raise it early rather than cancelling late. [Coordinating lesson dates](/help/scheduling-logistics/coordinate-lesson-dates) covers how to handle changes professionally.

## Zoom lessons

Online lessons remove travel entirely, but they add a setup burden. Get it right once and it stays right:

- **Use one consistent link per student, pinned in the chat.** The student should never have to search for a new meeting ID. Pinning the link in the chat means it survives the session scrolling past.
- **Test audio and screen share before Lesson 1.** Do a two-minute test call with the parent or student before the first session. Discovering your microphone is muted in minute one of a paid lesson is the fastest way to lose credibility.
- **Have a backup plan.** Know your phone hotspot password by heart, and agree with the parent that if the internet fails you will either reconnect within a few minutes or reschedule. Say this before it happens, not during.

## Equipment checklist

Before every online lesson, run through the basics:

| Item | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Microphone | Test it works; a headset beats a laptop mic |
| Internet | Stable enough for video and screen share |
| Space | Quiet, with a plain or tidy background |
| Materials | Worksheets or notes open and ready 10 minutes early |

The same materials discipline applies to in-home lessons: arrive with the lesson already planned, not planned on the student's dining table. The [delivery standards](/help/tutor-guide/delivery-standards) page sets out what MatchMax expects from every lesson, online or in person.

## Keep a shared folder per student

Create one shared folder per student, whether you teach on Zoom or in person, and keep your lesson notes, homework, and marked work there. Online students get a single place to look; in-home students and parents can still follow what was covered. A folder that is kept current is also the easiest evidence of progress when you write [post-lesson feedback](/help/parent-communication/post-lesson-feedback) to the parent.

Whichever mode you choose, consistency is what parents notice: same link, same arrival time, same folder. That reliability, not the technology, is what keeps a case going.
