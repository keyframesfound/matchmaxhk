Your child's first lesson works best when the small things are sorted before the tutor knocks on the door or the Zoom call starts. This checklist covers confirming the slot, setting up the space, and knowing what a good first lesson actually looks like.

## Confirm the slot with your tutor

Once you have been matched, your tutor will suggest a day and time. Confirm the details explicitly in one message: the date, the start and end time, the location (your home, the tutor's home, or Zoom), the lesson length, and the hourly rate. A quick recap avoids the classic "I thought it started at 4:30" mix-up. If the proposed slot no longer works, ask to move it as early as you can using the WhatsApp scripts in [Reschedule or cancel a lesson](/help/parent-scheduling/reschedule-or-cancel-a-lesson).

## Prepare the space

### Face-to-face / in-home lessons

Set up a quiet table away from the TV and away from where siblings are playing. Good lighting, a comfortable chair, and stationery (pencils, pens, eraser, ruler, and a notebook) are usually enough. Lay out the school textbooks and any recent test papers so the tutor can see them without rummaging through a school bag. If the lesson is at the tutor's home or a centre, plan the journey so your child arrives calm rather than rushed.

### Zoom lessons

Check the device, camera, and microphone the day before, and again ten minutes before the lesson. A tablet or laptop is easier for shared worksheets than a phone. Make sure the battery is charged or the charger is within reach, and use a stable Wi-Fi connection. Send the tutor the meeting link in advance and agree who creates the call. Headphones help children focus, especially in busy flats.

## What to have ready

Have recent school work on hand: the latest test papers, marked homework, and any school report cards. Know the name of the curriculum and level your child is studying, such as DSE, IB, IGCSE, or A Level, and the textbook or exam board if you know it. If you are not sure how these differ, see [Compare IB, DSE, IGCSE and A Level curricula](/help/parent-support/compare-ib-dse-igcse-and-a-level-curricula). It also helps to jot down two or three things you want the tutor to focus on, plus anything useful about how your child learns best.

## Set expectations with your child

Tell your child what will happen, who the tutor is, and how long the lesson lasts. Keep the tone low-pressure: the first lesson is a chance to meet and get a feel for each other, not a test to pass. For younger children, agree a quiet activity for siblings during the lesson, and decide whether you will sit nearby. You do not need to hover; if you have questions about supervision or handovers, read [Lesson safety and supervision guidelines](/help/parent-safety/lesson-safety-and-supervision-guidelines).

## What the first lesson typically covers

Expect rapport plus a diagnostic, not a full syllabus sprint. A good tutor will spend part of the lesson getting to know your child, then work through a short piece of school work or a few diagnostic questions to gauge the real level, strengths, and gaps. By the end you should have a sense of the starting point and a rough direction for the next lessons. Judge the fit after one or two lessons based on engagement and clear communication, not on how much content was covered in sixty minutes. From there, [Track your child's progress lesson by lesson](/help/parent-support/track-your-childs-progress-lesson-by-lesson) shows how to keep the momentum visible.
