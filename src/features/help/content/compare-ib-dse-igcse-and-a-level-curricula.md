Hong Kong families meet all four of these systems, sometimes within one household. This guide explains how each curriculum works and how it is assessed, so you can match a tutor's specialism to the system your child actually sits. It deliberately makes no claim about which is better; the right system is the one your school runs and your child suits.

## HKDSE

The Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education is the local secondary school examination, usually sat at the end of Secondary 6. Students take core subjects, including Chinese, English, Mathematics, and Liberal Studies or its successor, plus elective subjects chosen from a broad list. A distinctive feature is school-based assessment, where coursework and school-level tasks during the course contribute to the final result alongside the public written exams. Tutors who specialise in DSE tend to focus on past-paper technique, marking-scheme language, and the rhythm of school-based assessment deadlines.

## IB

The International Baccalaureate spans ages 3 to 19 through three programmes: the Primary Years Programme (PYP), the Middle Years Programme (MYP), and the Diploma Programme (DP), which most parents mean when they say "IB". It is inquiry-based, asking students to investigate and connect ideas rather than only recall content. In the DP, assessment combines internal assessments marked by teachers and moderated externally with final written exams at the end of the two-year course. Tutors working with IB students often concentrate on internal assessment drafts, extended essay and coursework structure, and exam technique for the final papers.

## IGCSE

The International General Certificate of Secondary Education, offered by exam boards such as Cambridge and Edexcel, is a subject-based qualification typically taken around ages 14 to 16. Students sit separate exams in each chosen subject, and results are grade-based per subject rather than a single diploma score. Because everything rides on the final written exams, tutors focus on syllabus coverage, past papers by board, and command words in exam questions, such as the difference between "describe" and "explain".

## A-Level

GCE A-Levels are the UK route taken in Sixth Form, typically at ages 16 to 18, and are also common in international schools in Hong Kong. Students specialise deeply in a small number of subjects. Depending on the exam board and specification, assessment is either modular, with AS and A2 units sat at different points, or linear, with all exams at the end of the course. Tutors focus on the specific board and specification, depth over breadth in each subject, and structured exam answers that hit assessment objectives.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Curriculum | Assessment style | Typical ages | What tutors focus on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HKDSE | Public written exams plus school-based assessment | 17 to 18 (Secondary 6) | Past-paper technique, marking-scheme language, SBA deadlines |
| IB (DP) | Internal assessments plus final written exams; inquiry-based | 16 to 19 (PYP from 3, MYP in between) | Internal assessment drafts, essay structure, final exam technique |
| IGCSE | Subject-based written exams set by boards such as Cambridge and Edexcel | 14 to 16 | Syllabus coverage per board, past papers, command words |
| A-Level / GCE | AS/A2 modular or linear end-of-course exams | 16 to 18 | Board-specific depth, assessment objectives, structured answers |
